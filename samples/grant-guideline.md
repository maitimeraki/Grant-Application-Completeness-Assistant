# Green Horizon Innovation Grant Programme 2026 — Call GH-2026-02

**Full call text for applicants.** Programme reference: GH-2026-02.
Issued by: National Climate & Innovation Agency (NCIA).
Version of call text: 2.1 (15 January 2026). This call text supersedes version 2.0.

---

## 1. Programme overview

The Green Horizon Innovation Grant Programme funds applied research and pilot
deployment projects that reduce greenhouse-gas emissions in small and medium
enterprises. Call GH-2026-02 is the second call of the 2026 funding year and
has a total budget of EUR 24,000,000.

Projects funded under this call must run between 12 and 24 months. Grants are
paid in instalments: 40% on signature, 40% on mid-term approval, 20% on final
report approval. The maximum grant per project is EUR 600,000.

## 2. Eligibility

### 2.1 Eligible applicants

To be eligible, applicants must satisfy **all** of the following conditions:

1. The applicant must be a legal entity established in a Member State of the
   European Economic Area. Proof of legal registration is required.
2. The applicant must have been actively operating for at least two full
   financial years before the call deadline. Two complete audited financial
   statements must be uploaded.
3. The applicant must employ at least 5 full-time equivalent (FTE) staff at the
   date of submission. A signed headcount declaration is required.
4. The applicant must not be classified as an enterprise in difficulty under
   EU state-aid rules. Applicants must complete Annex A1 (self-declaration).
5. Consortium applications must include at least two partners from at least
   two different Member States. A signed consortium agreement is mandatory
   when more than one partner is involved.
6. Single applicants may apply only if they are a medium-sized enterprise as
   defined in Recommendation 2003/361/EC.

### 2.2 Ineligible activities

The following activities are not eligible and will lead to exclusion:

- Activities that are commercial production or routine product installation
  without an explicit research component.
- Activities addressing fossil-fuel exploration or extraction.
- Activities that have already received funding for the same work package
  from any other public funding source. Declaration of other funding is
  required (Annex A3).
- VAT is not an eligible cost, except where it is non-recoverable under
  national law and the applicant provides written confirmation.

### 2.3 Eligibility check order

Eligibility is checked before quality evaluation. Applications that fail any
mandatory eligibility criterion in section 2.1 are rejected and are not
scored.

## 3. Submission requirements

### 3.1 Required documents

The following documents are **mandatory**. An application missing any
mandatory document is ineligible:

| # | Document | Format | Limit |
|---|----------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Completed application form (Parts A and B) | PDF, form fields filled | Part B max 10 pages |
| 2 | Detailed budget table with cost categories | XLSX or PDF | 1 sheet |
| 3 | Audited financial statements (2 most recent years) | PDF | no limit |
| 4 | Signed headcount declaration | PDF | 1 page |
| 5 | Legal registration certificate | PDF | 2 pages |
| 6 | Consortium agreement (consortium applications only) | PDF | no limit |
| 7 | Declaration of other public funding (Annex A3) | PDF | 2 pages |
| 8 | Letters of support from at least two end-user organisations | PDF | 1 page each |

The following documents are **recommended**. Their absence does not make the
application ineligible, but evaluators may consider it during scoring:

- A logic model or theory of change for the project (recommended).
- A data-management plan (recommended for projects generating primary data).
- Letters of intent from pilot sites (recommended for deployment projects).

### 3.2 Format rules

1. Part B must not exceed 10 pages. Font must be at least Arial 11 or
   Times New Roman 12, single spacing. Tables may use font size 10.
2. Applications exceeding the page limit will be **rejected without
   evaluation**.
3. All documents must be uploaded as searchable PDF files. Scanned
   signatures are accepted only on the consortium agreement and letters of
   support.
4. File names must follow the pattern `GH-2026-02_<Annex>_<Acronym>.pdf`.
5. The application must be submitted through the NCIA portal. Applications
   submitted by email are not accepted.

### 3.3 Deadline

The submission deadline is **31 March 2026, 17:00 CET (Brussels time)**. The
portal closes automatically at this time. Late submissions are rejected
without exception; the system clock of the portal is the authoritative
reference. Applicants are strongly advised to submit at least two hours
before the deadline.

## 4. Quality evaluation criteria

Applications that pass the eligibility check are evaluated by two
independent expert readers. Each criterion is scored 0–5. The thresholds are:

1. **Excellence of the innovation approach (0–5, threshold 3).** The
   approach must be novel beyond the state of the art and must include a
   verifiable research hypothesis or technical objective.
2. **Impact and measurement (0–5, threshold 3).** The application must
   specify quantified emission-reduction targets, a baseline, and a
   monitoring plan with named indicators.
3. **Implementation and consortium capability (0–5, threshold 3).** Work
   packages must have named responsible partners, milestones with dates,
   and realistic risk mitigation.
4. **Cost-efficiency and budget coherence (0–5, threshold 3).** Budget
   lines must be justified per work package and must not exceed 30% of the
   total for a single work package unless separately justified.

An application is funded only if **all four** criteria score at or above
their threshold and the weighted total is at least 15 of 20
(weighting: excellence ×1.5, impact ×1.5, implementation ×1,
cost-efficiency ×1).

Budget rules: personnel costs must use the daily-rate method; subcontracting
above 20% of the total budget requires written justification; equipment
purchases above EUR 50,000 require at least three quotations.

## 6. Reporting and compliance

1. Beneficiaries must submit a mid-term report and a final report using the
   NCIA reporting template. Reports received after the reporting deadline
   may be subject to a 5% budget holdback.
2. The final report must include audited expenditure and a statement of
   non-recovery of EU or national funding for the same costs.
3. Beneficiaries must publish at least one open-access publication or one
   openly licensed dataset resulting from the project.
4. Beneficiaries must acknowledge the programme in all communication
   material using the wording in Annex D.
5. On-the-spot checks may be carried out at any time during the project.
6. Breach of these obligations may lead to suspension or clawback of
   payments, without prejudice to national law.
